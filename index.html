What is Buffer for?

Buffer is money set aside for occasional or irregular costs during the cycle, such as a household purchase that doesn’t fit comfortably into a normal week. It is separate from Future Weeks, which holds the money planned for later weekly transfers. You decide when to use Buffer and move the money yourself in your banking app.

A MONTH AT A TIME

Built around
your cycle.

Spending Plan is geared around a monthly allowance, from one cycle start to the next. Your plan divides the spending pool into releases across the days it covers; the first is available at the start.

Choose your usual payday and the app suggests cycle dates. If that date falls on a Saturday or Sunday, it suggests the preceding Friday. If the day doesn’t exist in a month, it uses the last day of that month first.

Need a different plan for one month? If you’re paid early in December, for example, you can adjust that cycle’s dates and amounts and review the revised weekly releases before starting. Your saved defaults don’t have to change.

Bank holidays and individual payroll rules aren’t automatically accounted for. Check the suggested dates against when your money actually arrives and adjust them if needed.

GOOD TO KNOW

Questions,
answered.

Practical details for getting started and making the plan your own.

How do I set up my first plan?

Choose Set up a new plan to work through your banking setup, funding dates, allowance, Buffer and weekly timing. You can choose whether to use those answers as defaults for future plans. You can review the suggested amounts and dated releases before saving. Saving creates a Planning draft; it does not move money or start a cycle. After completing the bank setup, start the cycle separately. You can also run setup again from Settings.

What does “Available now” mean when setting up a cycle?

Available now is your first spending allocation, ready to use straight away. After moving the rest into Future Weeks and Buffer, leave this amount in your Spending account. There’s no extra transfer to make. Some versions call this amount “Keep”.

What bank accounts do I need?

Use two separate accounts: one for household income and bills, and one for everyday spending. Keep Future Weeks and Buffer outside your card-spendable balance, in pots, spaces or equivalent reserves. Your bank needs a way for you to separate and move those amounts yourself.

Does Spending Plan connect to my bank?

No. It does not connect to your bank, track transactions or see live balances. You make transfers in your banking app and record them in Spending Plan.

Can I change the plan during a cycle?

Yes. For an unexpected expense, Top up this week brings money forward from Future Weeks. If extra money becomes available, add it to Future Weeks; if you need money elsewhere, take some out. Review the revised releases before recording the change, and make any required movement in your bank yourself. Buffer remains separate.

What is Buffer for?

Buffer is a separate reserve for occasional or irregular costs during the cycle. Future Weeks is reserved for later weekly transfers. You decide when to use Buffer, move the money in your banking app and record its use in Spending Plan.

Does it work offline?

Yes. Your plan is stored locally and the app is fully useful offline. You do not need an app account.

What about iCloud?

Private iCloud plan sync is optional and off until you choose to enable it. When enabled, it uses your private iCloud account to sync plans. If versions conflict, they are retained for review rather than silently replacing one another.

How are weekly amounts worked out?

The weekly spending pool is divided according to the days covered by each release. Releases are whole pounds: proportional amounts are rounded down and the final release balances the pool. Any sub-pound planning remainder stays in Buffer. The first allocation is available at the start.

Can I choose which day my weekly spending money becomes available?

Yes. Under Dates and transfers → More timing options, choose Funding day for a simple schedule: the first amount is available when the cycle starts, followed by releases every seven days. Or choose a Fixed weekday to make weekly planning fit your routine. For example, if you’re paid on Friday, the first amount is available then and later releases can fall on Sundays. Short first or final periods receive an amount based on the days they cover, and the actual dates can mean more or fewer than the usual four or five allocations.

What does Release count mean?

Release count is the number of allocations in the cycle, including the first amount available at the start. Automatic is the easiest choice for most people: the app uses the cycle dates to work out the count. With Funding day timing, you can instead choose four or five if the dates allow: four means the first allocation plus three later releases; five means the first plus four. The schedule and amounts are recalculated, and Buffer may change if your four- and five-release targets differ. Fixed weekday timing always uses an automatic count.

What does Plan default mean?

Plan default uses the release-count preference saved with that plan, so you normally don’t need to choose it each cycle. You can make a different choice for one cycle without permanently changing the saved default.

Will changing these settings change how much money I have?

Changing timing doesn’t add money to or remove money from your allowance. It can change the release dates, the number of allocations and individual amounts; Buffer can also change when different release-count targets apply. Review the updated schedule before saving. Spending Plan calculates and records releases, but you move the money yourself in your banking app.

How does payday affect the cycle?

Choose a usual payday to get suggested monthly cycle dates. A Saturday or Sunday payday is suggested as the preceding Friday. If your payday falls beyond the end of a month, the app uses that month’s last day before applying the weekend rule. Bank holidays and individual payroll arrangements are not calculated automatically, so check and edit your dates when needed.

Can I change the plan for one month?

Yes. In Edit this month, you can adjust dates and amounts for that cycle and review the weekly releases before starting. For example, if you’re paid early in December, you can set dates that reflect when your money arrives without changing your saved defaults.

YOUR PLAN, YOUR BUSINESS

Private by design.

Your plan stays on your device unless you choose to enable private iCloud sync or export a backup. There are no app accounts, analytics, advertising or bank connections.

Exported backups contain financial plan data and are not encrypted by the app. Store them somewhere private. Please don’t include account numbers, passwords or sensitive financial details in support emails.

Optional iCloud sync uses Apple’s private iCloud service. Backups and device reminders are separate from sync.
